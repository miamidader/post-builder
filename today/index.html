Miami-Dade County Commissioners will take a final vote Tuesday, Nov. 17, on an ordinance reshaping the Miami-Dade Economic Advocacy Trust. Sponsored by Commissioner Oliver Gilbert, the measure directs the agency to shift its focus from Black residents to lower-income and economically distressed individuals across the county. The change comes weeks before state law SB 1134, signed by Governor Ron DeSantis to restrict government DEI efforts, takes effect. The Trust originated in 1983 following the Miami riots sparked by the death of Arthur McDuffie. Under Gilbert's proposal, the agency will keep running Teen Court and housing assistance for buyers earning up to 140 percent of the area median income, while ending trustee demographic mandates.

  1. DADE COUNTY WAS OFFICIALLY CREATED ON FEBRUARY 4 1836
  2. MIAMI AREA ROOTS TRACED BACK TO 1836
  3. HOW OUR REGION BECAME DADE COUNTY IN 1836

Dade County officially came into existence on February 4, 1836. The newly established county received its official name in honor of Major Francis L. Dade and the Miami River.

The year 1836 marked a defining chapter for the development of our area. In that exact same year, Fort Dallas was built right here along the frontier landscape.

The newly constructed Fort Dallas immediately served as an active military installation during the Second Seminole War. Together, the strategic military outpost and the formal designation of the county set the original groundwork for the historic footprint of our Miami community.
