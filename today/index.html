Miami-Dade voters will decide on Amendment 3 on the Nov. 3 ballot, a measure that would significantly increase Florida's homestead tax exemption for longtime residents.

If passed, the non-school exemption would jump from $50,000 to $150,000 in 2027, and reach $250,000 in 2028 for primary homeowners living in the state before the end of 2026. Florida TaxWatch estimates this change would save city of Miami homeowners $1,216 in 2027 and $2,448 in 2028.

However, the Florida Policy Institute projects Miami-Dade County would lose $1.5 billion across fiscal years 2028 and 2029. Local transit, trash pickup, libraries, and Jackson Health System, which projects a $64 million funding loss, could face cutbacks.

  3. THE 1836 ORIGINS OF DADE COUNTY AND FORT DALLAS

Dade County officially came into existence on February 4, 1836, marking the formal beginning of our local jurisdiction. The new county was named in honor of Major Francis L. Dade and the Miami River.

That same year, Fort Dallas was established along the riverbanks. The fort immediately took on a strategic defense role, operating as an active military base throughout the Second Seminole War.

From the historic grounds of Fort Dallas to the designation along the Miami River, the foundational events of 1836 established the local community and defined the early territory of Dade County.
