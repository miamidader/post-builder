A hypothetical ten-foot rise in ocean levels would put low streets across Miami Beach beneath everyday tides. Situated between the Atlantic Ocean and Biscayne Bay, low-lying ground would stay underwater without massive defensive upgrades. Seawalls alone cannot prevent flooding because South Florida rests on porous limestone, which lets rising groundwater bubble up from underneath. This rising water table compromises septic systems that require dry soil to process waste. Miami-Dade is already converting vulnerable septic tanks to sewer lines in Little River, working to protect communities and the Biscayne aquifer that provides our drinking water.
