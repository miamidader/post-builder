Miami Beach is rolling out a new 120-day voluntary pilot program designed to help local restaurants and food retailers redirect surplus food to community members in need. Sponsored by Commissioner Laura Dominguez and supported by Mayor Steven Meiner, the educational initiative links city kitchens with food recovery groups at no cost or liability to donors. Participating spots receive contact details for four partner organizations: Food Rescue US, Farm Share, Nourish Miami, and Feeding South Florida. Along with feeding local families facing food insecurity, the program aims to lower landfill methane emissions and cut pollution across the city. Businesses will also complete a brief survey detailing their experiences to shape future city food waste policies.

  3. HOW THE MIAMI RIVER HELPED NAME DADE COUNTY IN 1836

Dade County officially began on February 4, 1836. The county was named after Major Francis L. Dade and the Miami River, marking the formal start of our local geographic history.

During that same year of 1836, Fort Dallas was built in the area. The newly constructed outpost served as a military base during the Second Seminole War.

The year 1836 defined early regional history through the creation of Dade County, its naming after Major Francis L. Dade and the Miami River, and the strategic presence of the Fort Dallas military installation.
