Miami-Dade County anticipates losing hundreds of millions in tax revenue following the passage of Florida Amendment 3 on Nov. 3 with 61.98 percent voter approval. Taking effect Jan. 1, 2027, the measure elevates the non-school homestead exemption to $150,000 in 2027 and $250,000 in 2028. Local officials project the county will collect roughly $385.8 million less starting in October 2027, with reductions reaching $696.7 million by October 2028. Property taxes currently fund essential county operations, such as 73 fire rescue stations, meals for home-bound seniors, two animal shelters, 50 libraries, over 300 parks, and transit services including Metrorail and Metromover.

  2. SPEED LIMITS COMING FOR MIAMI DELIVERY ROBOTS
  3. ARE DELIVERY ROBOTS TAKING OVER YOUR MIAMI SIDEWALKS

Miami-Dade County commissioners will cast a final vote Nov. 17 on new regulations for sidewalk delivery robots. The proposal from Commissioner Micky Steinberg introduces operating permits and caps the devices at 8 mph on most sidewalks. Speed limits would drop to 6 mph in heavy pedestrian zones and reach 10 mph in crosswalks. The automated machines already operate in Wynwood, Brickell, downtown Miami, Coral Gables, and Miami Beach. Under the plan, devices cannot block transit stops or curb ramps and must limit idle time to 30 minutes. Violations carry fines up to $500. A county analysis by Deputy Mayor Roy Coley notes creating enforcement roles will cost $251,773 annually.
