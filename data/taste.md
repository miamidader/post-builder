# Scoring guide for @miamidader

This is the guide the system uses to score every story from 0 to 100 each morning. It is plain words on purpose: you can edit it any time, and it gets updated once a month using the numbers from your Instagram insights.

Version 1 (seed). Written from the first posts on the account and from what you have said. It is a starting guess, not proven. Do not treat any line as fact until the monthly numbers back it up.

## Audience

People who live in Miami-Dade County and care about what happens near them. Short, clear, local.

## Raise the score for

- Things that touch everyday life: prices, home sales, rent, traffic, transit, utilities, weather and health alerts, safety warnings for neighborhoods.
- Records, firsts and "largest, only, most" stories about Miami-Dade.
- Local pride: Heat, Hurricanes and Dolphins news, rankings that put Miami on top, big events like Art Basel.
- Surprise and curiosity: odd facts, unusual events, things people say "wait, really?" about.
- Stories reported by several outlets at once (the coverage number), because that usually means it is big.
- Fresh stories, under about 12 hours old.

## Lower the score for

- Routine government meetings, votes and budgets with no clear effect on residents.
- National or state politics with no direct Miami-Dade angle.
- Stories that are mostly a video or a photo gallery with no facts to write from.
- Event listings, "where to" guides and promotional pieces.
- Anything older than about 24 hours unless it is a big story.
- Sad or graphic stories that only say something terrible happened, with nothing readers can learn or act on.

## Open questions (to settle with data)

- Do crime and police stories get shared more or less than money and housing stories?
- Do posts with a circle photo beat posts without one?
- Does time of day matter?
- Do curiosity-style headlines beat straight-news headlines?
